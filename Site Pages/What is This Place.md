ComplexityCafe started off as my personal notes on complexity and complex systems. For various reasons I hope to cover, these notes just kept on growing and expanding into different areas. I then adopted Obsidian and source controlled the notes, shared them on a few different forums, and finally decided to turn them into a blog or live journal.

My goal of these notes started as a study of complex systems. This turned out to be a large project, in short, because of the rich intellectual history of issues of complexity in mainstream science, the many one-off discoveries and proto-sciences mostly in the 20th century, the founding and ongoing work of the Santa Fe institute and other institutions devoted to "core" issues of complexity, and the subsequent proliferation of complexity concepts in frameworks across the disciplines. It becomes very difficult outside of 2000-2012, give or take a few years, to "find" complex systems as a standalone focal point. The last major textbook with an attempt at nailing it down was by Thurner in 2018. In recent years core complexity appears to have largely dissolved. 

The goal of ComplexityCafe, in short, is to "find" complexity as it once existed as a single focal point of study. The approach is creating a single, unified place to trace the threads that became complex systems science, what it condensed into, and how it dispersed into various domains. From there, we hope to knit together people from various communities back to something resembling a mainline root for some of the concepts they may be contending with in their field and have a unified place to see the origin of these concepts, the foundational models and formulations of them, and where and how they are used elsewhere. Hopefully this spurs interest from disparate scientists in their field back into the roots of complexity and helps accomplish progress on the foundational questions.
