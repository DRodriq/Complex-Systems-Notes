# **Major Databases & Comparative Sets**

### **1. Binford’s Hunter-Gatherer Database**
- Compiled by Lewis Binford in _Constructing Frames of Reference_ (2001).
- ~339 hunter-gatherer societies described ethnographically.
- Includes environment type, mobility, group size, subsistence mix, storage, technology.
- A go-to resource for modeling how ecology shapes forager lifeways.
---
### **2. The Human Relations Area Files (HRAF)**
- Hosted at Yale University.
- Massive ethnographic database with coded variables on subsistence, ecology, kinship, and behavior across hundreds of societies.
- Includes hunter-gatherers, pastoralists, horticulturalists.
- Subscription required, but widely used in anthropological research.
---
### **3. eHRAF World Cultures & Archaeology**
- Online, searchable, coded ethnographic and archaeological collections.
- Lets you filter societies by environment type (savanna, arctic, desert, etc.) and subsistence.
- Useful for looking at both modern analogues and archaeological contexts.
---
### **4. Hunter-Gatherer Database (HGDB)**
- An ongoing digital project by archaeologists/anthropologists (Premo, Grove, others).
- Includes demographic, ecological, and social data for hunter-gatherer groups.
- Still patchy but growing, and often linked to agent-based modeling.
---
### **5. Seshat: Global History Databank**
- Covers a broader sweep (not just foragers), but includes data on early societies and environments.
- Used for cross-cultural modeling of complexity, warfare, cooperation, etc.
---
### **6. Palaeoenvironmental Databases**
These don’t track “societies” but the **climates and landscapes** in which they lived:
- **NOAA Paleoclimatology Database** (lake cores, speleothems, pollen).
- **PANGAEA Data Publisher for Earth & Environmental Science** (big climate datasets).
- **African Palaeoecology Database** (focused on East African Rift Valley environments).
