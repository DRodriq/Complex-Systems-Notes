Let’s brainstorm a set of ways this deficit in foundational complexity theory is limiting AI, with possible directions where a more rigorous complexity science could help:

---

### 1. **Black-Box Nature of Deep Learning**

- **Deficit:** We design architectures (transformers, CNNs, diffusion models) and tune hyperparameters largely through heuristics and brute-force experimentation, not grounded theory.
    
- **Complexity Angle:** A framework for _self-organization and attractor landscapes_ could help us understand why certain architectures stabilize, collapse, or suddenly generalize.
    

---

### 2. **Hyperparameter Chaos**

- **Deficit:** Small changes in learning rates, batch sizes, or initialization can swing performance unpredictably — a hallmark of sensitive dependence on initial conditions.
    
- **Complexity Angle:** Insights from nonlinear dynamics and bifurcation theory could guide principled ways to map stable vs. unstable learning regimes.
    

---

### 3. **Emergence Without Theory**

- **Deficit:** “Emergent abilities” of LLMs (like in-context learning or reasoning across domains) appear suddenly and unpredictably at scale.
    
- **Complexity Angle:** Complexity science has long studied _phase transitions_ in collective systems; a general framework might help us anticipate (not just observe) when capabilities emerge.
    

---

### 4. **Explainability as A Posteriori Science**

- **Deficit:** Unlike physics or engineering, where design is grounded in models, in AI we build first, then reverse-engineer what we made.
    
- **Complexity Angle:** A theory of _multi-level causation_ and _emergent patterns_ could let us design with interpretability in mind, rather than bolting it on afterwards.
    

---

### 5. **Scaling Laws Without Mechanistic Understanding**

- **Deficit:** Current “laws” of AI (e.g. Kaplan’s scaling laws) are empirical curve-fits, not derived from underlying principles.
    
- **Complexity Angle:** A deeper account of _network growth, resource distribution, and adaptive trade-offs_ could turn these rules of thumb into predictive theory.
