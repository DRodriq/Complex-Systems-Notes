# What this area is “called”

There isn’t a single established banner, but people approach it under different labels:

- **Computer Systems & Networks**
    - _Performance modeling, resource allocation, queuing theory, network economics._
    - Names: “systems modeling,” “network architecture,” “distributed systems economics.”
- **Complex Systems / Complex Adaptive Systems**
    - _Emergence, adaptation, agent-based modeling, network formation._
    - You’re explicitly using tools from here (fractal nodes, evolutionary dynamics, order parameters).
- **Computational Economics / Network Economics**
    - _Utility functions, pricing, congestion control, mechanism design._
    - “Market-based resource allocation,” “economic models of the Internet.”
- **Autonomic Computing / Self-Organizing Networks**
    - IBM (early 2000s) coined “autonomic computing”: systems that self-configure, self-heal, self-optimize.
    - Current names: “self-driving data centers,” “self-managing networks.”
- **Evolutionary Computing / Artificial Life**
    - _Emergence of strategies, node birth/death, mutation._
    - Very close to your “nodes can evolve, merge, split, die” mechanics.
- **Information Theory & Network Science**
    - _Measuring throughput, resilience, bottlenecks, centrality._
    - This is the “physics” layer of what you’re doing.

In short, what you’ve described could be called:  
**Complex Adaptive Computing Networks**  
 or **Evolvable Information Infrastructure Modeling**  
 or more simply, **Network-of-Networks Complexity Modeling**

# Who is working on things like this

### 1. **Complex Systems Institutes**
- **Santa Fe Institute (SFI)** — exactly the kind of “fractal definition, emergent specialization, order parameters” thinking.
- **New England Complex Systems Institute (NECSI)** — applies complexity to infrastructure and networks.
- Complexity science groups at universities: Indiana (CASOS at Carnegie Mellon), Northwestern’s Complex Systems group, Arizona State’s Complex Adaptive Systems Initiative.
### 2. **Systems & Networking Departments**
- **MIT CSAIL (Computer Science & AI Lab)** — especially groups on distributed systems, networking, cloud.
- **UC Berkeley EECS** — the Roofline model, cluster design, data center economics.
- **Stanford CS / EE** — networking economics, congestion control (e.g., Balaji Prabhakar, Nick McKeown).
- **UW, CMU, ETH Zurich, EPFL** — strong distributed systems and networking labs.

### 3. **AI Infrastructure Research**
- **Google Brain / DeepMind / Anthropic / OpenAI infrastructure teams** — they don’t publish all details, but they absolutely think in terms of data center scaling, throughput bottlenecks, emergent cluster behavior.
- **NVIDIA Research** — studies GPU cluster scaling and efficiency under workloads (close to your node definitions).
- **Microsoft Research (MSR Systems & Networking)** — work on self-managing data centers, global-scale services.

### 4. **Network Economics & Policy**
- **CAIDA (UC San Diego)** — Internet topology, economics, ISP peering models.
- **ICSI Berkeley** — Internet measurement, emergent properties.
- **Telecom/ISP research labs** (AT&T Labs, Bell Labs heritage) — network formation, traffic modeling.

### 5. **Artificial Life & Evolutionary Computation**
- **ALife community (MIT Press ALife journal, conferences)** — node birth/death, emergent specialization, self-organizing networks.
- **Complex Systems & Artificial Life at Binghamton (Sayama, who wrote the _Complex Systems Modeling_ text).**

# How to position what you’re doing
If you want to **ground it academically**, you could frame it as:
- “I’m modeling **computing infrastructure as a complex adaptive system**, where nodes, edges, and workloads obey physical ceilings, but strategies, roles, and even topology emerge dynamically under costs and user demand.”
That is legible both to:
- **Computer systems folks** (as an ambitious extension of performance modeling + economics)
- **Complex systems people** (as an applied case of adaptive networks)
- **Artificial life people** (as a synthetic ecology of digital organisms)
