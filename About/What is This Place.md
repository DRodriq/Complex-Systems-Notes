The goal of these notes started as a study of "complex systems," by way of the [[Santa Fe Institute]] and the large orbit of affiliates. This has expanded because of the rich intellectual history of issues of complexity in mainstream science, the many one-off discoveries and proto-sciences mostly in the 20th century, the founding and ongoing work of the Santa Fe institute and other institutions devoted to "core" issues of complexity, and the subsequent proliferation of complexity concepts in frameworks across the disciplines. It becomes very difficult outside of 2000-2012, give or take a few years, to "find" complex systems as a standalone focal point. The last major textbook with an attempt at nailing it down was by Thurner in 2018. In recent years core complexity appears to have largely dissolved. 

The new goal is to "find" complexity. The approach is creating a single, unified place to trace the threads that became complex systems science, what it condensed into, and how it dispersed into various domains. I want to knit together people from various communities back to something resembling a mainline root for some of the concepts they may be contending with in their field and have a unified place to see the origin of these concepts, the foundational models and formulations of them, and where and how they are used elsewhere.
