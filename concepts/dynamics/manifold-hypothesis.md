# Manifold Hypothesis

> The claim that high-dimensional data (such as text embeddings or images) does not uniformly fill its ambient space — it lies on or near a much lower-dimensional manifold embedded within that space. The true geometry of the data is the geometry of that manifold, not of the surrounding dimensions.

## Orientation

Real-world data generated by processes with a limited number of degrees of freedom will cluster near a low-dimensional surface even when represented in hundreds or thousands of dimensions. Estimating this *intrinsic dimensionality* — the effective number of independent directions on the manifold — is a prerequisite for understanding whether clustering, distance metrics, and dimensionality reduction are operating appropriately. Standard Euclidean distances in high-dimensional ambient space are a poor substitute for geodesic distances along the manifold.

## Connections

**Attractors** (`concepts/dynamics/attractors.md`) — strange attractors are low-dimensional manifolds in high-dimensional phase space; the manifold hypothesis is the same observation applied to data geometry.

**UMAP** (`concepts/computation/umap.md`) — UMAP operates by approximating the Riemannian geometry of the data manifold; it assumes the manifold hypothesis.

**Takens embedding** (`concepts/dynamics/takens-embedding.md`) — delay embedding reconstructs the attractor manifold from a scalar observable; the manifold hypothesis is what makes such reconstruction possible.

## Sources
