# Universality

## Orientation

The empirical and theoretical fact that systems with radically different
microscopic constituents exhibit identical critical behavior — same critical
exponents, same scaling functions — near their phase transitions. Systems
partition into *universality classes* determined only by symmetries and
dimensionality of the order parameter, not by the details of constituents.
Explained by the renormalization group: coarse-graining flows in
coupling-constant space converge to a small number of fixed points, each of
which defines a universality class.

## Connections

Universality is one of the deepest ideas in physics: it says that much of the
microscopic detail is irrelevant to macroscopic behavior near critical points.
Conceptually bridges to [[scaling-laws]] in biology and cities (West), to
[[scale-free-networks]] and other power-law phenomena, and to representation
learning (Mehta et al. argue RG and representation learning share structure).
