# Hunter-Gatherer Modeling

> Quantitative and agent-based modeling of forager societies — using ethnographic, archaeological, and palaeoenvironmental datasets to study how ecology shapes mobility, group size, subsistence, and social organization.

## Orientation

Hunter-gatherer societies are a workhorse subject for complexity-adjacent modeling: small, comparatively well-bounded populations whose lifeways are strongly coupled to environment, making them tractable for agent-based and demographic models. The field draws on a handful of large reference databases that allow cross-cultural and palaeoecological comparison.

## Reference Databases

- **Binford's Hunter-Gatherer Database** — ~339 societies; compiled by Lewis Binford in *Constructing Frames of Reference* (2001). Variables: environment, mobility, group size, subsistence mix, storage, technology.
- **Human Relations Area Files (HRAF)** — Yale-hosted ethnographic database with coded variables on subsistence, ecology, kinship, behavior. Subscription required; widely used.
- **eHRAF World Cultures & Archaeology** — searchable, coded ethnographic/archaeological collections; filter by environment type and subsistence.
- **Hunter-Gatherer Database (HGDB)** — ongoing project (Premo, Grove, others). Demographic, ecological, social data; often linked to ABM.
- **Seshat: Global History Databank** — broader sweep including early societies; cross-cultural modeling of complexity, warfare, cooperation.
- **NOAA Paleoclimatology Database** — palaeoenvironmental (lake cores, speleothems, pollen).
- **PANGAEA Data Publisher** — large palaeoclimate datasets.
- **African Palaeoecology Database** — East African Rift Valley environments.
