# Applied Ecology Domain

> The application of complexity science frameworks — nonlinear dynamics, CAS, network theory, ABM, scaling laws — to ecological systems: populations, communities, ecosystems, and evolutionary dynamics.

*Vault editorial stance and scope decisions: [[synthesis/why-complexity]].*

## What This Domain Covers

Ecology is one of the oldest and richest application domains for complexity science. Population dynamics, predator-prey interactions, community assembly, ecosystem stability, and evolutionary change are all inherently nonlinear, multi-scale, and emergent phenomena. The frameworks of nonlinear dynamics, CAS theory, network science, and ABM all have well-developed ecological applications.

For this vault, ecology matters primarily as an application domain that tests and extends the conceptual frameworks developed in complexity, dynamics, and computation — and as the domain where some of the most rigorous quantitative complexity science has been done (West's scaling laws, Kauffman's NK model applied to evolution, Bak-Sneppen punctuated equilibrium).

## Key Concepts From Other Domains Applied Here

- **Nonlinear dynamics** (`concepts/dynamics/`) — Lotka-Volterra is a classic nonlinear ODE system; its analysis is pure dynamical systems theory
- **Network science** (`concepts/networks/`) — food webs are networks; their structure (connectance, modularity) determines stability
- **ABM** (`concepts/complexity/agent-based-modeling.md`) — individual-based models in ecology; forager models, landscape models
- **Scaling** (`synthesis/scaling-thread.md`) — West's biological scaling; the 3/4 power law is the primary empirical success of the scaling program
