# Self-Organization in Non-Equilibrium Systems — Prigogine (1977)

> Prigogine and Nicolis' technical monograph establishing the theory of self-organization in non-equilibrium chemical and biological systems, presenting the mathematical framework for dissipative structures.

## Orientation
This is the rigorous companion to the popular "Order Out of Chaos." Prigogine and Nicolis develop the full mathematical theory of dissipative structures, using the Brusselator (a model chemical oscillator) as the primary example. The book establishes how far-from-equilibrium systems can undergo bifurcations that produce spatial and temporal order. It is the technical foundation of Prigogine's Nobel Prize work.

## Key Ideas
- Brusselator: a model chemical system exhibiting sustained oscillations and spatial patterns
- Turing instability in chemical systems: diffusion-driven symmetry breaking
- Mathematical analysis of bifurcations from thermodynamic equilibrium
- Chemical turbulence and chaos in reaction-diffusion systems
- Thermodynamic criteria for self-organization
