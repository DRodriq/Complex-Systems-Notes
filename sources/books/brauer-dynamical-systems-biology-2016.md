# Dynamical Systems for Biological Modeling: An Introduction — Brauer, Kribs (2016)

> An introductory text on dynamical systems theory with applications to biological systems including population dynamics and epidemiological modeling.

## Orientation
This textbook introduces the mathematical tools of dynamical systems — equilibrium analysis, stability, bifurcations, and limit cycles — through biologically motivated examples. Epidemiology, predator-prey systems, and population genetics serve as primary application domains. It is more applied and accessible than Hirsch-Smale, designed for students in mathematical biology.

## Key Ideas
- Equilibrium and stability analysis in biological contexts (SIR models, predator-prey)
- Phase plane analysis and limit cycles in ecological systems
- Bifurcations as models of disease outbreak thresholds and population crashes
- Basic reproduction number R0 as a key analytical concept
