# Exploring Complexity: An Introduction — Prigogine, Nicolis (1989)

> An accessible introduction to complexity and self-organization, co-authored by Nobel laureate Ilya Prigogine and Grégoire Nicolis, connecting thermodynamics of irreversible processes to the emergence of ordered structures.

## Orientation
This book introduces non-specialists to the deep connections Prigogine discovered between far-from-equilibrium thermodynamics and the spontaneous emergence of ordered structures. Concepts include dissipative structures, bifurcations in chemical systems (the Brusselator), and biological pattern formation. It bridges rigorous science and accessible exposition.

## Key Ideas
- Dissipative structures: order maintained by continuous energy flow far from equilibrium
- Near-equilibrium systems approach thermodynamic equilibrium; far-from-equilibrium systems can self-organize
- Bifurcation points as moments of symmetry breaking and emergent structure
- Bénard convection and the Belousov-Zhabotinsky reaction as classic examples
- Irreversibility and the arrow of time as fundamental, not secondary, phenomena
