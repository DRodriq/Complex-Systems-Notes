# Self-Organization in Biological Systems — Bonabeau (2001)

> A comprehensive treatment of self-organization in biological systems, co-authored with Guy Theraulaz and Jean-Louis Deneubourg, covering swarms, flocking, pattern formation, and colony organization.

## Orientation
This book examines how biological systems — from ant colonies to bird flocks to slime molds — produce complex organized behavior without central control. Bonabeau and colleagues provide both the theoretical frameworks (stigmergy, positive and negative feedback, random amplification) and empirical examples across taxa. It is the definitive reference for biological self-organization and swarm intelligence.

## Key Ideas
- Stigmergy: indirect coordination through environmental modification (ant pheromone trails)
- Swarm intelligence: collective problem-solving without individual-level intelligence
- Positive and negative feedback as amplifying and stabilizing forces in self-organization
- Pattern formation through reaction-diffusion and activator-inhibitor mechanisms
- Applications to robotics and distributed computing (swarm robotics)
