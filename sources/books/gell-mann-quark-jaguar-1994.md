# The Quark and the Jaguar — Gell-Mann (1994)

> Gell-Mann's exploration of the relationship between simplicity and complexity in nature, connecting quantum mechanics, information theory, and the behavior of complex adaptive systems.

## Orientation
Written by the Nobel-winning physicist and SFI co-founder Murray Gell-Mann, this book examines how fundamental physical laws (simple at the quark level) give rise to the astounding complexity of the living world (the jaguar). Gell-Mann introduces concepts of effective complexity, schema, and coarse-graining, arguing that complex adaptive systems share universal features discoverable through scientific inquiry.

## Key Ideas
- Effective complexity: the length of a concise description of regularities in a system
- Algorithmic information content vs. effective complexity
- Schema: internal models used by adaptive agents to interact with their environment
- Coarse-graining as the process by which macroscopic descriptions emerge
- The connection between the simple laws of physics and biological and cultural complexity
