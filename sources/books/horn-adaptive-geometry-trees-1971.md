# The Adaptive Geometry of Trees — Horn (1971)

> A classic ecological study arguing that the branching geometry and crown architecture of trees reflects optimization for light capture in specific competitive environments.

## Orientation
Horn's book applied optimization thinking to plant ecology decades before evolutionary game theory became standard. He showed that tree architecture — branching angles, leaf arrangement, crown shape — varies systematically between species occupying different light environments (sun vs. shade, gap vs. understory) in ways consistent with maximizing photosynthetic returns. It was an early example of applying optimality models to morphology.

## Key Ideas
- Tree crown shape and leaf arrangement as adaptations to competitive light environments
- Monolayer vs. multilayer leaf arrangement strategies
- Optimization framework applied to plant morphology
- Successional dynamics reflected in tree geometry: pioneer vs. climax species architectures
