# The End of Certainty — Prigogine (1997)

> Prigogine's late philosophical work arguing that irreversibility, probability, and the arrow of time must be incorporated as fundamental features of physical law, not eliminated as approximations.

## Orientation
In this final major work, Prigogine argues that classical and quantum physics' obsession with time-reversible laws has been a mistake. Irreversibility is fundamental, not emergent. Chaos, quantum mechanics, and far-from-equilibrium processes all point to a universe in which probability and time's arrow are built in at the most basic level. The book is philosophically provocative and scientifically ambitious.

## Key Ideas
- Time's arrow is fundamental, not a statistical artifact
- Chaos at the classical level makes trajectories intrinsically probabilistic
- Quantum mechanics requires non-unitary evolution at the fundamental level
- "End of certainty": deterministic trajectories give way to probabilistic distributions
- A new dialogue between science and the humanities about time
