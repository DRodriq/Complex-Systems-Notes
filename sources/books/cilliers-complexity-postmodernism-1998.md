# Complexity and Postmodernism — Cilliers (1998)

> A philosophical examination of complexity theory through the lens of postmodern thought, arguing that complex systems and postmodern epistemology share deep structural similarities.

## Orientation
Cilliers makes a provocative argument that complex systems — particularly neural network models — instantiate postmodern ideas about distributed meaning, non-representational knowledge, and context-dependence. He draws on Derrida and Lyotard to illuminate what complexity means philosophically, and uses complexity to give rigor to postmodern claims. The work occupies a niche intersection of philosophy, cognitive science, and systems theory.

## Key Ideas
- Complex systems store knowledge distributedly, not in localized symbols
- Meaning is context-dependent and emergent — resonant with Derridean différance
- Distinction between complicated and complex systems
- Neural networks as models of complex, non-representational cognition
- Complexity challenges reductionism and linear causality
