# Complexity Economics — Arthur, Beinhocker, Stanger (2020)

> A collection bringing together key contributions to complexity economics, arguing that economies are non-equilibrium systems shaped by increasing returns, path dependence, and evolutionary dynamics.

## Orientation
This volume compiles foundational and recent work in complexity economics, primarily associated with W. Brian Arthur and the SFI economics program. It challenges neoclassical equilibrium economics with a view of markets as complex adaptive systems that constantly evolve, lock in to sub-optimal paths, and are shaped by positive feedback and network effects.

## Key Ideas
- Increasing returns and positive feedback produce path dependence and lock-in
- Economies are non-equilibrium, evolutionary systems — not static optimization machines
- Technology evolution follows combinatorial, ecologies-like dynamics
- Agent heterogeneity and bounded rationality as fundamental assumptions
- Emergence of market structures from micro-level agent interactions
