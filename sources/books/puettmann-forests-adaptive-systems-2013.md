# Managing Forests as Complex Adaptive Systems — Puettmann, Messier (2013)

> A book applying complex adaptive systems theory to forest management, arguing that forests should be managed as inherently complex, adaptive systems rather than as simple production machines.

## Orientation
Puettmann and Messier bring CAS concepts — emergence, resilience, self-organization, nonlinearity — into forest management practice. They critique traditional forestry's linear, control-based approach and propose management strategies that work with complexity rather than against it: maintaining diversity, allowing for surprise, and managing for resilience.

## Key Ideas
- Forests as complex adaptive systems with nonlinear dynamics and emergent properties
- Resilience thinking: managing for the capacity to absorb disturbance
- Diversity as a source of adaptive capacity
- Silviculture that mimics natural disturbance regimes
- Critique of monoculture and maximum sustained yield approaches
