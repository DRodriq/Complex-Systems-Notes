# Synergetics: An Introduction — Haken (1983)

> Hermann Haken's foundational text on synergetics, the theory of self-organization in physical, chemical, and biological systems, introducing the concept of order parameters and the slaving principle.

## Orientation
Haken developed synergetics as an overarching theory of self-organization, parallel to and partly overlapping with Prigogine's dissipative structures. The key insight is the slaving principle: near a bifurcation, fast degrees of freedom are "slaved" to slow order parameters, massively reducing the system's effective dimensionality. The laser is Haken's paradigm example. The theory applies to neural systems, fluid dynamics, and pattern formation broadly.

## Key Ideas
- Order parameters: slow, collective variables that describe macroscopic pattern
- Slaving principle: fast subsystems adiabatically follow slow order parameter dynamics
- Bifurcations as the mechanism of spontaneous symmetry breaking and pattern emergence
- Laser as a paradigmatic self-organizing system (from incoherent photons to coherent light)
- Applications to visual cortex pattern formation and morphogenesis
