# Artificial Life — Langton (1987)

> The proceedings of the first Artificial Life workshop at Los Alamos, establishing artificial life as a research program studying life-as-it-could-be through computational and synthetic systems.

## Orientation
This volume — edited by Christopher Langton and emerging from the 1987 Los Alamos workshop — launched Artificial Life as a field. It gathered researchers exploring self-organization, self-replication, evolutionary computation, and emergent complexity in synthetic systems. The work sits at the intersection of biology, computation, and complexity science.

## Key Ideas
- Life-as-it-could-be: studying life by building it, not just observing it
- Self-replication and von Neumann universal constructors
- Cellular automata as substrates for life-like behavior
- Edge of chaos hypothesis: life and computation occur at the phase transition between order and disorder
- Evolutionary dynamics in computational systems
