# Increasing Returns and Path Dependence in the Economy — Arthur (1994)

> A collection of Arthur's influential papers arguing that increasing returns and positive feedback produce path dependence and market lock-in, challenging the neoclassical assumption of diminishing returns.

## Orientation
Arthur's collected papers established a new economic paradigm where positive feedbacks — network effects, learning curves, infrastructure costs — cause markets to lock into outcomes that may not be optimal. The QWERTY keyboard and VHS vs. Betamax became canonical examples. This work was foundational for complexity economics and technology management.

## Key Ideas
- Increasing returns: each unit produced or adopted makes the next more likely
- Path dependence: history matters — early random events can lock in long-term outcomes
- Multiple equilibria: no single optimal outcome, but several possible attractors
- Technology competition as a complex adaptive process
- Positive feedback networks in economic systems
