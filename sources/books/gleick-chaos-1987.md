# Chaos: Making a New Science — Gleick (1987)

> A landmark popular account of the chaos revolution in science, tracing how a generation of researchers discovered that deterministic systems could produce wild, unpredictable behavior governed by hidden order.

## Orientation
Gleick chronicles the emergence of chaos theory through the stories of scientists like Edward Lorenz, Mitchell Feigenbaum, Benoit Mandelbrot, and Robert May. The book covers sensitive dependence on initial conditions, fractal geometry, and the universality of routes to chaos. It is a primary historical document of how nonlinear dynamics became a recognized scientific field in the 1970s–80s.

## Key Ideas
- Sensitive dependence on initial conditions (the "butterfly effect") makes long-term prediction impossible in many deterministic systems
- Strange attractors give chaotic systems geometric structure in phase space
- Feigenbaum's universality: period-doubling routes to chaos follow universal constants across wildly different systems
- Fractal geometry (Mandelbrot) provides the mathematical language for chaotic structures
- Chaos unified researchers across physics, biology, chemistry, and mathematics
