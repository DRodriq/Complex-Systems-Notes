# The Origins of Order: Self-Organization and Selection in Evolution — Kauffman (1993)

> Kauffman's comprehensive technical work presenting the theoretical foundations of self-organization in biology, arguing that much of biological order arises spontaneously rather than through natural selection alone.

## Orientation
This dense, mathematically rich monograph is Kauffman's magnum opus. It covers Boolean network models of gene regulatory networks, NK fitness landscape theory, autocatalytic set theory for the origin of life, and the edge-of-chaos hypothesis. Kauffman argues rigorously that self-organization produces a "order for free" that natural selection then shapes, overturning the view that selection alone explains biological complexity.

## Key Ideas
- Boolean networks (N-K networks): random gene regulatory networks self-organize to ordered, near-critical dynamics
- NK fitness landscapes: tunable-ruggedness model of evolutionary search
- Autocatalytic sets: collectively autocatalytic chemical networks as models of life's origin
- Edge of chaos: NK networks and biological systems evolve to the boundary between frozen and chaotic regimes
- Coevolution to the edge of chaos via coupled fitness landscapes
