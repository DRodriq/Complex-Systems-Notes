# Making Things Work — Bar-Yam (2004)

> An applied complexity science book arguing that failures in healthcare, education, military operations, and management arise from a mismatch between the complexity of the problem and the complexity of the solution.

## Orientation
Bar-Yam's accessible application of complexity theory to real-world systems. The central argument — complexity mismatch — holds that organizations and interventions fail when their internal complexity is too low to match the complexity of the system they are trying to manage. A top-down, low-complexity management structure cannot effectively control a high-complexity system. This principle is developed across several domains: healthcare, education, military command, and corporate management.

## Key Ideas
- Complexity mismatch: the complexity of a managing system must match the complexity of the managed system (derived from Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety)
- Healthcare failures as a complexity problem — overly centralized systems cannot handle individual variation
- Military command: effectiveness of small autonomous units vs. rigid hierarchies in high-complexity environments
- Education: one-size-fits-all approaches fail because student needs are high-complexity
- Practical design principle: increase solution complexity or reduce problem complexity to restore alignment
