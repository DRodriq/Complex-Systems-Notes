# Mathematical Models in Biology — Edelstein-Keshet (2005)

> A classic textbook in mathematical biology, covering population dynamics, enzyme kinetics, pattern formation, and spatially extended biological systems using differential equations.

## Orientation
Edelstein-Keshet's textbook has become a standard reference for mathematical biology, offering rigorous yet accessible coverage of ODE and PDE models in biological contexts. Topics span single-species population models, predator-prey systems, reaction-diffusion equations (Turing patterns), and neuroscience applications. It is both a learning text and a research reference.

## Key Ideas
- Single-species population models: logistic growth, Allee effects, harvesting
- Predator-prey dynamics: Lotka-Volterra and extensions
- Enzyme kinetics: Michaelis-Menten and quasi-steady-state approximation
- Pattern formation via reaction-diffusion systems (Turing instability)
- Spatial models: diffusion, traveling waves, invasion fronts
