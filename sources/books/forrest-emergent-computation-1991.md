# Emergent Computation: Self-Organizing, Collective, and Cooperative Phenomena in Natural and Artificial Computing Networks — Forrest (1991)

> An edited volume exploring how computation and complex behavior emerge from the collective activity of simple processing elements, spanning immune systems, neural networks, and genetic algorithms.

## Orientation
This collection examines the idea that computation is not just a tool for studying complex systems, but itself an emergent phenomenon arising in natural and artificial networks. Edited by Stephanie Forrest at SFI, it covers immune system models, classifier systems, and evolutionary algorithms as examples of emergent, distributed computation.

## Key Ideas
- Distributed computation as an emergent property of interacting components
- Artificial immune systems as adaptive, self-organizing computational systems
- Classifier systems (Holland's framework) as emergent learning architectures
- Collective intelligence without central control
