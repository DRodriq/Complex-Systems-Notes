# The Economy as an Evolving Complex System — Anderson, Arrow, Pines (1987)

> The proceedings of the first Santa Fe Institute economics workshop, gathering physicists and economists to apply complex systems ideas to economic theory.

## Orientation
This landmark volume from SFI's founding economics workshops brought together Nobel-winning economists (Arrow) with physicists (Anderson) and other scientists. It represents the founding moment of complexity economics, challenging equilibrium theory with ideas from spin glass physics, evolutionary biology, and complexity science. Kenneth Arrow and Philip Anderson are the central intellectual figures.

## Key Ideas
- Economy as a non-equilibrium evolving system, not a static optimization
- Spin glass analogy: economies as frustrated systems with many local optima
- Evolution and adaptation rather than optimization as the appropriate metaphor
- Emergent economic order from local interactions
- Questioning of the rational expectations hypothesis
