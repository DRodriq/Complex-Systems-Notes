# Scale: The Universal Laws of Growth, Innovation, and Sustainability — West (2017)

> West's synthesis of his decades of work on scaling laws in biology, cities, and companies, arguing that universal mathematical regularities govern growth, metabolism, and innovation across radically different complex systems.

## Orientation
West presents the case that scaling laws — power-law relationships between size and various properties — reveal deep universal principles about how complex systems work. Biological organisms, cities, and companies all follow predictable scaling relationships that emerge from the network structures through which they distribute resources. The book is ambitious in scope, connecting metabolism, urban economics, and corporate dynamics under one framework.

## Key Ideas
- Quarter-power scaling in biology: metabolic rate scales as mass^(3/4)
- Cities scale superlinearly: doubling city size produces more than double innovation and productivity
- Companies scale sublinearly like organisms: slower growth and eventual death
- The common cause: network geometry optimizes for resource distribution
- Implications for urban planning, sustainability, and economic growth
