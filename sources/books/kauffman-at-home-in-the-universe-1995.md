# At Home in the Universe — Kauffman (1995)

> A popular account of Kauffman's argument that self-organization is a fundamental source of biological order alongside natural selection, suggesting life was not improbable but nearly inevitable.

## Orientation
Kauffman presents his major theoretical contributions — NK fitness landscapes, autocatalytic sets, the edge of chaos, and the "adjacent possible" — for a general audience. He argues that complex systems spontaneously generate order, and that natural selection acts on this pre-existing order rather than building all structure from scratch. The book is more accessible than his technical work in [[kauffman-origins-of-order-1993]].

## Key Ideas
- Self-organization generates "order for free" independent of selection
- NK model: fitness landscapes with tunable ruggedness
- Autocatalytic sets as a model for the origin of life
- Edge of chaos: complex adaptive systems evolve to the boundary between order and disorder
- The "adjacent possible" constrains and enables evolutionary trajectories
