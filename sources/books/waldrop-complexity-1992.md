# Complexity: The Emerging Science at the Edge of Order and Chaos — Waldrop (1992)

> A narrative account of the founding of the Santa Fe Institute and the emergence of complexity science, told through profiles of researchers like Holland, Kauffman, and Arthur.

## Orientation
Waldrop's book is the defining popular history of complexity science's birth at SFI. It traces how physicists, economists, biologists, and computer scientists converged on shared questions about emergent order, adaptation, and self-organization. The book profiles John Holland, Stuart Kauffman, W. Brian Arthur, Chris Langton, and others, showing how their ideas intersected at SFI in the 1980s.

## Key Ideas
- Complexity science as a unified inquiry cutting across disciplines
- Emergence: system-level properties arising from component interactions
- Complex adaptive systems as the central organizing concept
- Economic complexity: markets as evolving complex systems (W. Brian Arthur)
- SFI's interdisciplinary model as itself a complex adaptive experiment
