# Order Out of Chaos — Prigogine (1984)

> Prigogine and Stengers' landmark popular work arguing that the arrow of time and irreversibility are fundamental features of nature, not mere approximations, and that order spontaneously emerges far from equilibrium.

## Orientation
Written with Isabelle Stengers, this book presents Prigogine's Nobel-winning work on dissipative structures for a broad scientific audience. It argues that classical physics' time-reversibility is an idealization, that irreversibility is fundamental, and that ordered structures — from Bénard cells to living organisms — arise spontaneously in systems driven far from equilibrium. Philosophically ambitious, it challenges the Newtonian worldview.

## Key Ideas
- Dissipative structures: stable patterns maintained by continuous energy dissipation
- The arrow of time: irreversibility is fundamental, not illusory
- Far-from-equilibrium systems can spontaneously organize
- Bifurcations as choice points where history matters (symmetry breaking)
- Dialogue between physics and other sciences about time and complexity
