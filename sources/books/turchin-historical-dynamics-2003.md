# Historical Dynamics — Turchin (2003)

> Turchin's foundational work applying mathematical modeling and dynamical systems theory to historical processes, introducing the field he later called cliodynamics.

## Orientation
Turchin applies the tools of population ecology and dynamical systems — differential equations, cycles, bifurcations — to the study of historical empires and state formation. Drawing on the Ibn Khaldun tradition and modern data, he models the rise and fall of empires as driven by population dynamics, elite overproduction, and asabiya (social cohesion). The book established rigorous mathematical history.

## Key Ideas
- Secular cycles: multi-century oscillations in population, state power, and social stability
- Asabiya (collective solidarity) as a quantifiable driver of state formation
- Metaethnic frontier hypothesis: border regions generate high-cohesion groups
- Mathematical modeling of historical processes using ODEs
- Empirical testing of historical theories against data
