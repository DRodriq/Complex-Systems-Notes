# Emergence: Contemporary Readings in Philosophy and Science — Bedau, Humphreys (2008)

> An anthology collecting key philosophical and scientific texts on emergence, covering both the conceptual distinctions and empirical examples of how higher-level phenomena arise from lower-level interactions.

## Orientation
This collection brings together classic and contemporary papers on emergence from philosophy of mind, philosophy of science, physics, and biology. Bedau's own distinction between "weak" and "strong" emergence is central, as are contributions from Jaegwon Kim (supervenience), Philip Anderson ("More is Different"), and others. It is the definitive reference anthology for philosophical work on emergence.

## Key Ideas
- Weak emergence: macroscopic patterns deducible from micro-level rules, but only by simulation
- Strong emergence: macroscopic causation not reducible to micro-level facts (contentious)
- Supervenience and downward causation as key conceptual problems
- Anderson's "More is Different" as founding text for emergence in physics
- Emergence in living systems, consciousness, and social phenomena
